"Welcome back to another fabulously hot day for leaving Scotomaville! You remember the physicist Stephen Hawking? "Multiplication builds curves, addition builds lines. Curves are powerful. Knowing how a curve is created and being able to spot them is critical to Leaving Scotomaville. Let's do a quick refresher on the difference between linear and exponential."

"Linear is like addition and let's apply this to a business, for example. Let's say in the first month of sales, you've gotten three new customers. And you work really hard to add three more customers the following month. You do the same thing for the third month in a row and you've added three additional customers. That means nine, okay? Exponential- a little different. Let's say you have the same effort where you have three sales in your first period. But you teach these people to make referrals and each of those three give you three referrals. And they've taught the same thing, more referrals. Then, what you end up with over that is 3 times 3 is 9. 3 times 9 is 27, obviously more. But let's have a look at that on a graph to see what it looks like because the visual on it is what's important.

"So let's say that this is the number of weeks that go by. And over here on the side, this is 10,20,30 customers. So let's plot the linear end of the first period, you have 3. Into the second period 6. Into the third period 9. And here's that straight line, you started with 0. And so you would expect the future to look just like that, given the same linear addition. However an exponential, where you've taught someone else to do it or in some way multiplied rather than addition, in the first you start with zero. But in the first period, you have three equal and the second period, you have nine here and on the third period, you have 27- clear up here. This exponential looks like a curve, so at the end of a performance period, you can see that this is doing good. But you can see that biology and nature has learned to outperform addition because, obviously, far more. The best takeaway from this episode for you is to multiply. Don't add, don't subtract, multiply."
