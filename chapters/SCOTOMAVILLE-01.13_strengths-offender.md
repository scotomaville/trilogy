The Strengths we rely on in the marketplaceturn out to be our coping mechanisms - the way we offend those closest to us.

Today we’re talking about ‘strengths offender.’
We’re going to talk about how the thing that makesyou-you, that strength that you have persevered to develop that becomes your identity to find some anchor of who you are known as - that very thing that gives you value in the market - that’s your strength!
We’re going to talk about how to find it and thenhow to slay it! Because you see, it turns out that your strength is a coping mechanism from yourchildhood wounds.
Here comes the sheriff’s helicopter.
Man, is it low and noisy right over my head. Talkabout a demonstration strength? It’s offensive!

Google for the Gallup store andtake the top 5 Clifton Strengths test for 20 bucks.
How Our Strengths Shape Us The assessment essentiallyrecords your ‘reactive’ behavior.
You know, the first and fastestanswer for a whole series of questions. You want to take theassessment to reap a vocabu lary that gives you distinctionand a bridge to other Explorers.
The reason it’s so important toknow your strengths is not for you. It’s for everyone aroundyou! It’s a scotoma on how we feel offended, and we need thelabels to talk about it.
Offending with Our Strengths We consider our strengths tobe ‘NORMAL,’ but our strengths set the bar so high for everyonearound us that that’s how we offend everyone! My ‘Achiever’ isway above everybody else’s!
It’s not what we find out aboutour strengths. It’s the scotoma of our strengths. It’s that ourstrengths are precisely how we wear other people out, how wewound them! It’s how I get that glazed look over everyone when I start tarking... start tarking?
Oh, that’s an Asperger moment!
I get a glazed look over people’sfaces when I start talking! Of course, I’m acting ‘normal,’ butit’s too much for most people!
Achiever, input, learner, that’s a Vulcan mind-meld!
That’s unfair to do to everybody - but it’s my routine.
Strengths in Tough Times How do you think an achiever input - learner would deal with the lock-down of a global pandemic? I’d create an edu-taining video series using whatever tools I have, aiming to help someonelearn something incredible!
The unfortunate part of knowingyour strengths is that you’ll start seeing them emerge all the time.
They will be the obstacles, thechallenges, the things that frus trate you while summiting your Personal Everest.
I want to do things in less timethan it takes. I want to go fur ther, higher, do more becausethat got childhood attention.
Like you, I formed beliefs fromdysfunctional things in my family of origin. I believed that my onlyvalue came from being able to DO! Not who I became or who I am. I DID for others and with others that gave me meaning and purpose, and identity.
Whenever I get frustrated atthings not going as I imagined, I get more leverage; I press a littleharder, I get more done in less time. I speed up my words, andother people can’t understand me as well. So I habitually frustrate and offend others - like the Star Trek character Spock.
My strengths are great duringa global pandemic. I can shoot a video series and take a ‘Master Class,’ giving Angelina time away from me.
I can dig in and scrub a toiletand fix stuff BEFORE it breaks.
That’s an achiever. That’s how I can easily offend other people.

Leaving SCOTOMAVILLE 117 The Path to Self-Mastery Getting out of Scotomavillerequires a ‘test’ to find out what your strengths are, and self-assessment on how they’re being used to wound others, and howthey betray your aim at self-mas tery and personal growth.

Learning new ways of copingwith things, learning new skills, and not relying on the thingsthat you have tens of thousands of hours of practice with - that’sa tough one. It takes a lot of time, a lot of effort, a lot ofstumbles being triggered and taking the trash out and goingfor a walk and peeling the onion and figuring out where it camefrom - and then changing that mis-belief into an adult belief.
Doing so forced those myelinated sheaths to decay, moving the effort toward building newpathways, new thoughts, and then into new habits where theoutcomes are ones that YOU have designed!
Well, that is a lot! There’s a pattern in there of what you can do when you know your strengths.

The leading frustration I have extends from mytop strength of ACHIEVER. I expect we should have been in our new Airstream four to fiveweeks ago! I can’t make it happen. It’s out of my control. Everything I can muster is not enough. Itseems the obstacle is the way.
So it becomes my torment, the very thing thatstirs me up the most inside. I can’t use my strength to produce a different outcome. Itdoesn’t work. What about changing my thoughts about the obstacle?
The experience I need to learn from is rightin front of me. But I can’t use my strengths to change it. I can’t dig my way out of a global pandemic to deliver a 30-foot Airstream, even though it’s sitting in the warehouse. So I have to bepatient and wait, but patience is NOT something an ACHIEVER wants.

I will address. I recorded it in this episodeand watched myself saying I’m NOT patient because I am an Achiever. That’s why editingyour story is a powerful awareness mirror.
