A water bearer in India had two large pots, each hung on each end of a pole which he carried across his neck. Kevin Baerg'S Goldfish

Kevin Berg is the author of 'Created For Excellence' and 'Created To Win'. He was an early client of Angelina and I in Tacoma. He would come over evenings to work on his website with us. He shared one of his experiences as a young man that made a big impact on me.

He had won a goldfish at the fair during summer break in school. And after taking it home and becoming 'kid friends' he had to go to summer camp. He didn't want to leave his goldfish orphaned, so he grabbed the bowl and off he went to summer camp.

There were lots of things for the kids to do. He couldn't take that goldfish to all of the different activities and events. But the camp happened to have a lake and a dock.

He got the idea that he could let that goldfish go in a big pond. What a great idea. He would liberate that goldfish and let it out of the bowl that it was outgrowing.

He carried that goldfish proudly down to the dock and sat down with his bare feet in the water. He leaned over and carefully poured the goldfish into the pond. Within moments and to his horror the goldfish was swimming around in circles, not going out into the lake.

He tried with his shadow and shoo it into a different course, but before the goldfish understood, a large mouth bass saw it as easy dinner. Splash! No more goldfish.

Everything Kevin had dreamed of doing -of being the liberator - was turned upside down by the reality of having put that inexperienced and naive goldfish at risk. It was habitually going around and around and doing the same old thing, not recognizing opportunity nor danger.

You can imagine that no matter how much you want to help someone, if they're not ready and willing to go outside of their comfort, like that goldfish, then you are actually endangering them. They're easy prey for someone.

So my career of creating leverage for ideas to greatly expand on the internet, and the belief that allowing everyone to have access to everyone else is not necessarily a good thing in the real world. I had to learn that lesson the hard way on several projects that we took on over the following decades.
